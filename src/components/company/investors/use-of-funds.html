Use of funds

Where the money goes.

Most of the round goes into the product, because the product is the business: there is no cloud to build and no model to train. The rest goes into reaching teams, passing their security reviews, and running the company.

On screen · Approvals and permissions

Where the security money goes: per-tool permissions, and Terminal, Browser and Remote control off until the user switches them on.

See how it works
  • Product and engineering
    55%

    The self-hosted edition, multi-agent routines, the Chrome extension and the desktop app. The four open roles on the careers page.

  • Go-to-market
    25%

    Demos, the Chrome Web Store, the students program, content and the first sales hire for regulated teams.

  • Security, compliance and self-hosting
    10%

    Security reviews, an external audit, the air-gapped build and support tooling for teams on their own servers.

  • Operations and legal
    10%

    Company running costs, contracts, accounting and the data room.

A plan, not a promise: the split moves with what the first paying teams ask for.

What each part unlocks

Engineering

The self-hosted edition ships, and the extension and the desktop app leave beta.

Go-to-market

The demo list turns into paying seats, and the Chrome Web Store brings the next users.

Security

Regulated teams can pass Zod through their review, which is the gate to enterprise revenue.

Operations

The company can sign, invoice and support teams without the founder doing all of it.
