Revenue forecast

The next five years, on one page.

Revenue grows with seats. Each year adds a way to sell more of them: first individuals, then teams on their own server, then enterprises and a marketplace. The full financial model, with assumptions, is in the data room; this page is the shape of it.

On screen · Connectors

The connector catalogue is the seed of the Year 3 marketplace: apps, servers and packs that others can build and sell.

See how it works
  1. Year 1 · 2026

    Out of beta

    • Public launch of the desktop app and the extension
    • The Personal plan: the first paid seats
    • Listed in the Chrome Web Store
    • Revenue: Personal seats
  2. Year 2 · 2027

    Teams on their own server

    • The self-hosted Team edition, one container and one volume
    • Enterprise pilots in accounting, clinics and law with Books
    • Multi-agent collaboration in routines
    • Revenue: Personal plus Team seats
  3. Year 3 · 2028

    Enterprise and a marketplace

    • Air-gapped Enterprise with support agreements
    • A marketplace of connectors, skills and routines with a revenue share
    • Partners build modules on Books
    • Revenue: seats, support and marketplace share
  4. Years 4 to 5 · 2029 to 2030

    The default on-premise agent

    • The standard choice for an agent that runs on your own computer or server
    • Channel partners in regulated fields
    • Renewals carry most of the revenue
    • Revenue: renewals, enterprise and marketplace

What drives the numbers

Seats

Personal seats first, then Team seats that come in groups and renew yearly. One team is worth many individuals.

Margin

No inference and no cloud to run, so gross margin stays close to that of a desktop software license.

Renewals

Memory, routines and vaults accumulate in the user’s own files. The longer someone uses Zod, the more it knows, and the likelier the renewal.
